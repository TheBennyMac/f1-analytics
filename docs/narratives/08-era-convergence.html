Three-Era Convergence

Does the field always take 2–3 years to compress after a regulation reset?

The narrative

“After a major regulation change, one team always runs away with it for 2–3 years before the field converges.”

The claim implies a consistent structural pattern: regulation resets reliably produce multi-year dominance before parity returns. Testing this requires data across multiple eras — specifically Year 1 of each era, where the divergence (or lack of it) is most visible.

Method

Constructor points are tracked cumulatively round by round. At each round, the gap between P1 and P10 in the standings measures how spread the field is. A rising gap means the leader is pulling away; a flat or falling gap means the field is compressing.

import plotly.graph_objects as go

ERA_STYLES = {
    'Hybrid Power Unit Era': {'color': '#7B68EE', 'dash': 'solid',  'width': 2.5},
    'Ground Effect Era':     {'color': '#00D2BE', 'dash': 'solid',  'width': 2.5},
    '2026 Era':              {'color': '#FF8000', 'dash': 'dot',    'width': 2.5},
}

fig = go.Figure()

for era_name, style in ERA_STYLES.items():
    era_data = convergence_df[convergence_df['era_name'] == era_name].sort_values('round')
    if era_data.empty:
        continue
    season = era_data['season'].iloc[0]
    label = f"{season} ({era_name.replace(' Era', '')})"

    fig.add_trace(go.Scatter(
        x=era_data['round'],
        y=era_data['gap_p1_pn'],
        mode='lines+markers',
        name=label,
        line=dict(color=style['color'], width=style['width'], dash=style['dash']),
        marker=dict(size=5),
        hovertemplate=(
            f"<b>{label}</b><br>"
            "Round %{x}<br>"
            "P1–P10 gap: %{y:.0f} pts<extra></extra>"
        ),
    ))

fig.update_layout(
    title='Constructor Points Spread (P1–P10) — Year 1 by Era',
    xaxis_title='Round',
    yaxis_title='Points gap (P1 minus P10)',
    template='plotly_dark',
    legend=dict(
        orientation='h',
        yanchor='top',
        y=-0.15,
        xanchor='center',
        x=0.5,
    ),
    height=500,
    margin=dict(b=100),
)
fig.show()

Constructor points gap (P1 to P10) by round. A rising line means the leader is pulling away from the midfield; a flatter line indicates more competitive compression. The 2026 line will extend as rounds are added.

Findings

Question Finding
How spread was the 2014 (Hybrid Y1) field by season end? P1–P10 gap: 701 pts. Mercedes dominant throughout — Red Bull 296 pts behind.
How spread was the 2022 (Ground Effect Y1) field by season end? P1–P10 gap: 716 pts. Red Bull dominant from mid-season — similar scale to 2014.
How is 2026 tracking after 5 rounds? P1–P10 gap: 185 pts after R5. Directional only — too early to compare shape.
Is the divergence trajectory similar across eras? Yes at high level: both completed eras show a gap opening from Round 1 and widening monotonically. Rate and scale are similar.
Do Gini coefficients confirm this? 2014 final Gini: 0.598; 2022 final Gini: 0.562. Both moderate-high inequality — neither era was especially close at season end.

Verdict: Partially supported — on two completed eras.

Both the 2014 and 2022 regulation resets produced Year 1 seasons with significant and growing points spreads. The “one team runs away” pattern appears in both cases, though the dominant team differs (Mercedes in 2014, Red Bull in 2022). Whether this is a structural regulation-reset effect or coincidence requires more eras.

The 2026 spread after 5 rounds is tracking below the same-point spread in both prior eras — at Round 5, 2014 had a gap of ~154 pts and 2022 had ~172 pts, versus ~185 pts in 2026. Too early to draw conclusions; revisit at Round 10.
